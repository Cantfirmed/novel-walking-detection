Interactive explorer for Kang et al., Sensors 2018 (sensors-18-00297) using 3D gyroscope data
📐 IMU Gyroscope Variables & Coordinate Frame

A 3-axis MEMS gyroscope inside a smartphone or wearable measures angular rotation velocity around each body axis. All values in the CSV files are recorded in radians per second (rad/s) (where 1 rad/s ≈ 57.3 deg/s):

Variable Physical Meaning Units Description & Biomechanical Context
wx (ωx) Rotation around X-axis rad/s Lateral angular velocity (tilt / pitch across the screen width). In front trouser pockets, ωx or ωy often captures thigh forward/backward pendulum swing during footsteps.
wy (ωy) Rotation around Y-axis rad/s Longitudinal angular velocity (along the vertical length of the phone). Prominently stimulated when swinging arms or carrying the smartphone in hand.
wz (ωz) Rotation around Z-axis rad/s Yaw / twist velocity perpendicular to the screen face. Captures upper body or hip rotation and corner turning during walking.
||ω|| (Norm) Euclidean Magnitude rad/s Composite rotational speed: √(wx² + wy² + wz²). Represents total rotational intensity regardless of smartphone orientation.
time (t) Elapsed Time seconds (s) Timestamp recorded at nominal 100 Hz (Δt ≈ 9.9 ms). Linearly resampled to 20 Hz (50 ms) to match the paper's specification.
🔬 Algorithm Parameters & Equations (Sensors 2018 Paper)
Parameter / Variable Paper Default Function & Algorithmic Rationale
fs (Sampling Rate) 20 Hz Resampling frequency chosen by the authors to minimize battery power while satisfying Shannon-Nyquist (> 2 × max human walking cadence ≈ 2 Hz).
N (Window Length) 64 samples (3.2 s) Power-of-two size for fast radix-2 FFT. Spans 3.2 s (∼2 gait cycles) yielding discrete frequency bins Δf = 0.3125 Hz.
Sliding Step 1.2 s (24 samples) Time increment between consecutive windows. Corresponds roughly to the duration of one step/stride.
Sensitive Axis (Eq. 1) max ∑ |ωa(i)| Picks the axis with the highest L1 norm within the 3.2s window. Overcomes arbitrary smartphone orientation without needing costly 3D attitude alignment.
ω̄c (Walking Band) 0.6 Hz – 2.0 Hz Average FFT spectral amplitude across the human walking cadence band (bins k = 2, 3, 4, 5, 6). Contains the primary gait harmonics.
ω̄0 (Low-Freq Band) 0.0 Hz – 0.6 Hz Average FFT spectral amplitude in the sub-walking band (bins k = 0, 1). Captures constant bias, sensor drift, and gradual posture shifts.
τ (Min Amplitude Threshold) 10.0 (Eq. 5) Threshold for ω̄c. Distinguishes genuine walking from low-amplitude stationary tasks (like standing while typing or texting).
f̂w (Peak Cadence) Argmax of 4th-deg poly 4th-order polynomial A(f) = af&sup4; + bf³ + cf² + df + e (Eq. 7) is fitted through the 5 walking bins to interpolate the peak frequency without bin-quantization error.
α (Smoothing Factor) 0.80 (Eq. 8) Exponential moving average weight: f̄w,i = α·f̄w,i-1 + (1-α)·f̂w,i. Dampens single-window anomalies.
c (Step Accumulation) t × f̄w × 2 (Eq. 6) Total steps are accumulated by multiplying continuous walking duration by cadence. (Factor 2.0 converts strides to footsteps).

The algorithm resamples 3D gyroscope data to 20 Hz and shifts a 3.2s sliding window (64 samples) by 1.2s. Walking is identified via frequency-domain FFT features, and continuous cadence is fitted with a 4th-order polynomial:

ωx, ωy, ωz: 3-axis angular rates (rad/s)
||ω||: Total rotational magnitude √(ωx²+ωy²+ωz²)
Sensitive Axis: Axis with max ∑|ωa| (Eq. 1)
ω̄c: Mean energy in walking band (0.6–2.0 Hz)
ω̄0: Low-frequency baseline drift (0–0.6 Hz)
Walking Decision: ω̄c > ω̄0 (Eq. 4) AND ω̄c > 10 (Eq. 5)
Curve Fit: Argmax of 4th-deg polynomial → f̂w (Eq. 7)
Accumulated Steps: c = duration × f̄w × 2 (Eq. 6)
